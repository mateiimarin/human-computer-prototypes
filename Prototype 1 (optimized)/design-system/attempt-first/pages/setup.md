# Session setup

Read `../MASTER.md` first. Use the same tokens and component states.

**Student's question:** How should the tutor help me?

## Composition

Centered 760 px maximum column; selected exercise remains visible as a compact context row. Heading: “Choose how you'd like help.” Label this a preference choice, not an ability test.

Use two short sections, with visible question labels and radio-card answers:

1. Assistance: “Questions only” or “Questions + up to 3 hints.” Start with Questions only selected. Plain-language descriptions explain the difference.
2. Starting approach: “Start with a blank attempt” or “Start with a scaffold.” Blank is selected initially. A scaffold is a sequence of prompts, not completed reasoning.

A small optional preference for welcoming starter guidance can be a labeled Switch if the team uses that architecture input; omit it if it has no visible effect. Keep the agreement summary directly beneath the choices: permitted help, allowance, and “Final answers stay hidden.” This is a quiet paper-colored inset, not a green success banner.

Finish with Back and one yellow “Start working” action. All choices fit on one page on desktop; stack naturally on smaller screens. Avoid a multi-page quiz for these few preferences.

## Conflict and return states

If scaffold is selected while hints are off, keep that selection visible and explain: “Hints are off in your agreement. Begin with a starter question, or change your assistance choice.” Provide explicit “Use a starter question” and “Change assistance” actions. Do not silently turn hints on or make the student guess why Continue fails.

Back preserves preferences. Returning after work has started shows applied choices; an increase now follows the workspace's deliberate change flow. It must not use setup as a bypass around the pause or replenish consumed hints.

## Check

Student can describe what the tutor can provide, how many hints are allowed, and what stays hidden before entering the workspace. Radio groups work with keyboard arrows; labels and descriptions are programmatically connected.
